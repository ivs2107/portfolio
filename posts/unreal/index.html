Unreal

Introduction

This game was made with the goal of learning Unreal Engine. I played a lot of Nier Automata and was fascinated to know how big games are made and wanted also to try in Unreal how they do that. I really wnated to challenge myself.

Demo Gameplay

States

I made a state system to track the player’s current actions, such as attacking, dodging, taking damage, and other gameplay interactions.

Combo system

In Nier: Automata, you can chain light and heavy attacks to create combos, so I implemented something similar in the game. This lets players string moves together for more fluid and satisfying combat, making fights feel more dynamic and fun.

AI

I implemented AI for melee enemies, ranged enemies, and even the boss. Each enemy has simple behaviors, like chasing the player, attacking, or launching a projectile.

Boss

I created a boss with two phases. When it reaches mid-life, it duplicates itself and triggers a short cinematic.

Berserk mode

Just like in Nier: Automata, I added a berserk mode where the player becomes faster, deals more damage, and can chain combos more quickly.

Particles effects

I used particle effects to experiment with Unreal’s systems and polish certain parts of the game, like explosions, hits, and other visual feedback.

// example of effect in unreal

Reality Scan

I wanted to add something fun to the game, so I scanned one of my colleagues’ heads and turned it into a boss. To do this, I used RealityScan, which is a 3D scan tool that allows you to create 3D models from real-world objects using photos.

AI Voice

For the voice lines, I recreated the voice from Nier: Automata using AI voice generation and used it in the game. This allowed me to quickly generate dialogue and add more personality to some moments without needing to record voice actors for a fun project and keep the authencity of nier automata.
